## Education makes one a better person. Do you agree?
### Education makes one a better person, because it exposes students to diverse perspectives which can lead to a more compassionate and understanding individual.
Most education systems around the world expose students to multiple subjects, and many include a form of compulsory ethics or citizenship classes. This is because one of the primary goals of education itself is to impart in students good morals and values, such that they become good citizens of society. Such ethics classes expose students to moral and ethical reasoning, as well as case studies and examples, so that students have a chance to think through about the effects of decisions on various groups of individuals. These classes teach students to think beyond making decisions based on immediate self-gain, and consider the effects of their own actions on other people. This helps students make decisions that are more morally right. For example, every Singapore student is required to go through years of citizenship classes. During these classes, students are taught to be considerate of others, taught basic social etiquette, as well as taught to consider the viewpoints of people from all walks of life, including people of different races and religions. It is thus ingrained in Singaporeans growing up that such practices are correct, leading to a generally law-abiding and inclusive society. <br>
Additionally, academic subjects taught such as history and biology tend to include segments that make students aware of the effects of decisions made on others. Most common biology syllabus, such as GCSE Biology, teaches about mutation risk as a result of gene editing. This helps impart in students the ethical consequences that may arise from irresponsible use of technical tools. History students are often taught about various atrocities of war crimes in the past. Such classes nudge students toward realising what is morally wrong and right, allowing them to build better character, and be more compassionate to other people.

### Critics argue that education does not make one a better person, because it also imparts students with improved technical ability to create and weaponise tools that cause widespread suffering.
Education provides individuals with advanced technical knowledge and skills, and such knowledge in a highly-trained individual can be easily weaponised for self-serving purposes that lead to disastrous consequences to others. In contrast, a person who is uneducated may not have learnt the necessary skillsets that could propagate the harms of their crimes to a comparably large extent that an educated person could. For example, during the United States’ Opioid crisis, consultants from McKinsey & Company played a key advisory role that furthered the harms of drug abuse. These consultants had elite degrees from the world’s top business schools and were clearly well-educated. However, they advised a pharmaceutical company called Purdue Pharma to turbocharge oxycontin sales during a known addiction pandemic, by directly telling doctors that OxyContin was not very addictive. This led to more people getting prescribed OxyContin freely. With more people exposed to the drug frequently, they became addicted to it. As a result of their addiction, more people resorted to illegal means of obtaining the drugs, usually in heightened doses, which eventually led to their death. This goes to show that even with the high levels of education the consultants had, they were not necessarily better people. Instead, they used the knowledge they obtained from education to further financial gains, instead of thinking about real-life ethical consequences. It resulted in them making decisions that led to the death of many. <br>
Another example is where complex financial instruments like credit default swaps and Collateralised Debt Obligations (CDO) were found to fuel the 2008 Global Financial Crisis. These systems were innovated by quantitative analysts who were highly educated and had advanced degrees in Math and Physics. When investment banks bought such CDOs, they unknowingly bought more subprime mortgages that were opaquely bunched into the same category as highly rated, safe mortgages through the system. With the unstable interest rates of the subprime mortgages, it eventually ballooned to a value that became unpayable for many, leading to people defaulting on their loans. This eventually caused many large financial institutions to be at risk of collapse and created a debt issue. Therefore, this shows that education actually provides people with the enhanced ability to direct their creativity toward designing sophisticated mechanisms of greed that cause widespread suffering rather than good. Hence, education does not necessarily make one a better person.
